## Course Overview

FYEC100 is the First Year Experience Course. It is a three-credit Year 1 course offered in Semester 1. The course supports new COSTAATT students as they transition into college-level learning, understand COSTAATT's teaching and learning context, take responsibility for learning, and use support structures effectively.

FYEC100 is designed to promote student success and institutional goals such as improved retention, graduation, time to completion, and academic performance.

Source: FYEC100 Course Outline - Course Overview

## Delivery Mode and Moodle

FYEC100 is fully online and delivered asynchronously through the Moodle/eClassroom platform. Course materials, learning activities, assessment activities, readings, videos, journal activities, and submission links are administered through Moodle/eClassroom. Zoom or other virtual live sessions may support learning; recordings may be posted in the eClassroom.

- Rubrics and grading criteria where posted.

If a student cannot access Moodle, cannot locate a submission link, or has a technical access issue, refer the student to the LMS administrator or official student support process.

## Course Aims and Learning Outcomes

FYEC100 helps students:

- Understand teaching and learning at COSTAATT.
- Recognize their own learning philosophy and learning style.
- Take responsibility for learning and programme management.
- Understand programme structures, core curriculum, electives, support courses, and academic guidance.
- Access academic and student support structures.
- Use goal setting, time management, stress management, and conflict resolution strategies.
- Maintain good health and wellness.
- Work independently and collaboratively.
- Demonstrate ethical standards, academic integrity, respect, and social responsibility.
- Develop, manage, and present a reflective portfolio.

Upon completion, students should be able to manage their learning, evaluate sources, identify learning challenges, access support, communicate new learning, connect their goals to COSTAATT graduate attributes, produce work that reflects academic standards, and develop a reflective portfolio.

Source: FYEC100 Course Outline - Aims and Learning Outcomes

## Assessment Model

FYEC100 is assessed by coursework only. The course culminates in a portfolio.

Assessment weighting:

- Journals: 36% total, made up of 6 journal entries at 6% each.
- E-portfolio artifacts: 48% total, made up of 6 module e-portfolio activities at 8% each.
- Final e-portfolio: 16%.

Each module is complete when it is assessed. Students should use the relevant rubrics as a guide for journals, e-portfolio artifacts, and the final e-portfolio.

The assistant may explain these weightings and expectations. The assistant must not calculate official grades, predict marks, or replace lecturer feedback.

Source: FYEC100 Course Outline - Assessment

## Rubric Guidance

Reflective writing/journal entries are assessed using criteria such as:

- Connection to the course module.
- Clarity of writing.
- Grammar and mechanics.
- Overall reflection and personal insight.

E-portfolio activities/artifacts are assessed using criteria such as:

- Following artifact production instructions.
- Captioning that is clear, relevant, and creative.
- Grammar and mechanics.
- Clarity of writing.

The final e-portfolio is assessed using criteria such as:

- Design and layout.
- Organization and navigation.
- Clarity of writing.
- Grammar and mechanics.

The assistant may help students understand rubric language and revise their own work against general criteria, but must not assign a mark or say what grade the student will receive.

## Reflective Writing Guidance

FYEC100 journal/reflective writing asks students to think about what they experienced and learned in each module. Reflective writing is personal and should use the student's own experiences, thoughts, feelings, and learning.

A journal entry should include three components:

1. Description: Briefly look back at the module, event, session, activity, or experience. Keep this short, usually about 3 to 4 lines. Identify key concepts and ideas.
2. Analysis and interpretation: Think about the module in more depth. Consider what felt meaningful, significant, important, useful, or challenging. Connect the module to real-life experiences, group work, personal development, or future learning. This section may be about 6 to 8 lines.
3. Outcome: Explain what the module meant for the student's development as a learner or professional, what was learned, what could change, why it matters, and what the student will do next. This section may be about 5 to 6 lines.

Reflective writing is normally written in the first person and past tense, such as "I felt that..." or "I realised that..."

The assistant may help a student plan a reflection, brainstorm guiding questions, understand the three-part structure, or improve clarity. The assistant must not write the journal entry for the student.

Source: Reflective Writing Guide - Three Components

## E-Portfolio Guidance

An e-portfolio is a digital collection of course-related work such as assignments, reflective pieces, screenshots, images, videos, posters, artifacts, captions, and other evidence of learning. In FYEC100, the e-portfolio helps students document and present evidence of learning.

The preferred e-portfolio platform for FYEC100 is Google Sites because students can access it through their student portal. Students with experience may use another suitable website/e-portfolio tool if permitted by their lecturer.

Students should:

- Create an introductory or home page.
- Include a short "About me" section with name, programme, reasons for pursuing academic goals at COSTAATT, and an appropriate image.
- Explain the overall goals of the portfolio.
- Create separate pages or views for the course modules.
- Label sections clearly and logically.
- Include required artifacts and captions.
- Link pages/views so navigation is easy.
- Copy and submit the e-portfolio URL in the designated Moodle/eClassroom area.

Source: FYEC100 E-Portfolio Guide - E-Portfolio Setup

## Artifacts, Captions, and Permissions

Artifacts may be spelled `artifacts` or `artefacts`; both refer to evidence included in the e-portfolio. Each artifact should be chosen for a clear purpose. Students should record why they selected the artifact and turn that reason into a caption.

A useful caption explains:

- What the artifact is.
- Why it was selected.
- How it connects to the module or course learning outcomes.
- What it shows about the student's learning.

Students should save artifacts in a designated place such as Google Drive and set appropriate viewing permissions. A common sharing approach is `anyone with the link`, but students should follow lecturer instructions and consider privacy. If the lecturer can open the portfolio but not an artifact, the artifact may still have restricted Google Drive permissions.

Troubleshooting guidance:

- If the lecturer cannot access the e-portfolio, check the published-site sharing settings, publish again, copy the correct link, and send or submit the link as instructed.
- If the lecturer can open the portfolio but cannot open some content, check the permissions for the Google Drive files used inside the portfolio.
- A student may use screenshots inserted directly into the portfolio when appropriate and permitted.

Source: FYEC100 E-Portfolio Guide - Sharing and Permissions

## Academic Integrity and AI Use

COSTAATT supports appropriate and ethical integration of AI into teaching and learning. Students and lecturers are expected to manage AI use transparently at the course and assignment level.

Students should:

- Submit authentic and original work.
- Use citations and referencing to attribute sources and AI use.
- Ask the lecturer what AI use is permitted for a specific assignment.
- Use Turnitin where available to check and self-correct work before submission.
- Submit required originality or Turnitin documentation when instructed.
- Accept that non-compliance may lead to penalties or academic dishonesty referral depending on severity and evidence.

Lecturers should:

- Declare the permissible and impermissible levels of AI use in course and assignment instructions.
- Reiterate AI-use expectations for each assignment.
- Declare any AI/plagiarism detection tools used.
- Provide constructive feedback on infractions or missed opportunities.
- Use rubrics and course-level guidance to support transparency.

Source: COSTAATT AI Statement - Student and Lecturer Responsibilities

## COSTAATT AI Use Levels

COSTAATT's AI guidance describes five levels of AI use for assignments:

1. No AI Use: AI is not to be used at any point in the assignment or activity. Students rely on their own knowledge, understanding, and skills.
2. AI-Assisted Idea Generation and Structuring: AI may be used for brainstorming, structuring, and idea generation, but no AI content should be included in the final submission.
3. AI-Assisted Editing: AI may be used to improve grammar, spelling, clarity, and final output, but not to generate content. Original work with no AI content may need to be submitted as an appendix if required.
4. AI Task Completion with Human Evaluation and Oversight: AI may be used for specified components or tasks, but AI-generated content must be clearly identified, cited, and critically reviewed by the student.
5. Full AI with Human Evaluation and Oversight: AI may be used throughout the assignment as directed by the lecturer, with citations and human critical oversight.

Students should not assume a level is allowed. They should check the assignment instructions and ask the lecturer when unsure.

Source: COSTAATT AI Statement - AI Use Levels

## Turnitin, AI Detection, and Originality

Turnitin and other AI/plagiarism detection tools may be used as formative tools to help students and lecturers identify areas for citation, paraphrasing, discussion, or correction. There is no single universal percentage that automatically determines acceptable or unacceptable AI use or similarity; thresholds and interpretation may vary by course and discipline.

Students may be required to provide evidence of authentic content creation, such as early drafts, plans, notes, or versions, especially where AI use is suspected or likely.

The assistant must not interpret a Turnitin score as an official misconduct decision or tell a student whether they will be penalized. It may advise the student to review sources, cite properly, keep drafts, and speak with the lecturer.

Source: COSTAATT AI Statement - Turnitin and AI Detection

## AI Citation Guidance

The COSTAATT AI statement gives an example APA-style reference for ChatGPT:

OpenAI. (2023). ChatGPT (Mar 14 version) [Large language model]. https://chat.openai.com/chat

Students should follow the referencing standard recommended by their lecturer or faculty. Prompts and AI-generated text may need to be included in quotations or disclosed as instructed.

The assistant may explain that AI use should be cited and disclosed, but it should not invent course-specific citation requirements beyond the supplied policy and lecturer instructions.

Source: COSTAATT AI Statement - Citing AI

## Guardrails for Student Assistance

The assistant may:

- Explain FYEC100 course expectations.
- Summarize assessment weights and module expectations.
- Help students understand journal and e-portfolio instructions.
- Provide reflective writing prompts and planning questions.
- Help students make their own outline.

The assistant must not:

- Create final answers that the student can submit as their own.
- Grade work, predict marks, or claim official evaluation authority.
- Treat historical 2024 schedule dates as current deadlines.
